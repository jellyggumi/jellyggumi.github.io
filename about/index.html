About this site

This family site grew from two things we kept doing at home: explaining the small parts of Korean life to friends, and trying not to forget how quickly our daughter was growing. One half is a set of practical guides for people visiting Korea or settling in. The other is a diary for INa, known at home as Jelly GGumi. We keep the focus on family stories rather than a child's identifying details.

Who writes it

I am INa's parent, writing from Korea between the ordinary parts of family life. A post may begin with wet weather, a bus transfer, a baby class or a celebration we suddenly realised needed planning. The practical details are then checked against the public body, operator or institution responsible for them rather than copied from another travel summary.

That is also the limit of what this site claims. It is a personal account, not an official source. It does not give legal, medical, visa or financial advice, and where a detail is likely to change — a fare, a price, an opening time — the guides deliberately describe how something works rather than quoting a number that will be wrong within a year.

What you will find

  • Cultural tips — etiquette, holidays, names and forms of address, and the conventions that most reliably catch visitors out.
  • Food and dining — how a Korean meal is actually structured, what to order, and the regional dishes worth travelling for.
  • Travel and transport — getting around, the seasons, palaces, temples and the river.
  • Daily life — the practical machinery of living here: convenience stores, connectivity, waste rules, shopping.
  • Personal family records — dated milestones and family decisions, kept separate from general cultural guides.

How it is written

Each guide takes one subject and tries to answer it properly rather than broadly: what the thing is, how it works, why it works that way, and what a newcomer gets wrong about it. Korean terms are given in Hangul with a romanisation, because the romanisation alone is often useless for actually reading a sign.

AI may assist research, structure and an early English draft. It is never treated as a source and it does not invent a family event or photograph. Every article carries its source list and an editorial disclosure; the complete workflow is in the editorial policy.

New posts appear when the research is solid or a family story feels worth keeping. Corrections are genuinely welcome — if something here is wrong or has gone out of date, please tell me. I will check it, fix it and date the change.

Where else this is published

Most of the day-to-day writing happens in Korean on Naver Blog, Korea's largest blogging platform. That archive is indexed on the Naver page. Photographs from the family diary are collected in the albums, and there are a couple of browser games built alongside all of this.
