Practical guides

Korea, one useful little question at a time.

Start with a decision

Which answer do you need first?

A rule, a forecast and a museum label need different kinds of checking. These reading paths explain what each guide adds and where to verify the part that can change. The dates inside the articles identify their source checks; this index does not renew those checks.

Before paying, renting or selling

A bank limit, a broker's ceiling and a resale restriction are three different obstacles. Check the one attached to your transaction before treating a general explanation as permission.

  • A transfer stops at the account limit. Follow the distinction between opening an account and proving its purpose. Confirm the release documents with your own bank.
  • A property agent quotes a fee. Work through the deposit-plus-rent example, then check the provincial rate table. A maximum is not automatically an agreed price.
  • You want to list something secondhand. Check the item restrictions and the distinction between a pilot and a general permission before relying on another seller's listing.

Where the final answer lives: your bank, the relevant provincial fee table, and the regulator or platform responsible for the item.

Before choosing a travel date

A calendar date does not tell you whether a business opens, a tree has changed colour or a weather warning applies. Keep those questions separate.

  • Your phone receives a Korean alert. Learn the warning terms and message types, then read the issuing agency's current instructions.
  • Two foliage forecasts disagree. Compare what each forecast measures before choosing which one fits the visit you want.
  • A holiday falls on Saturday. Use the 2026 Chuseok example to understand why substitute-day rules differ by holiday; it is not a timetable for an individual venue.

Where the final answer lives: Korea Meteorological Administration alerts, the forecast publisher, and the venue or transport operator you will use.

When a cultural label puzzles you

Start with the exact Korean term or collection record. A familiar English label can hide a distinction that matters more than a broad introduction.

  • A celadon title names more than one technique. Read four museum records side by side: a title's technique word does not exclude another technique on the same object.
  • An old heritage number disappears. Separate the designation from the retired numbering system before assuming the object's status changed.
  • A Korean expression sounds like a baduk move. Compare the dictionary's board and everyday senses: a familiar metaphor is not automatically proof of a word's history.

Where the final answer lives: the National Museum of Korea collection record, the Korea Heritage Service designation record, and the cited law or Korean dictionary entry.

Before making a longer-term plan

An admissions route, a sponsor's promise and an insurance decision apply to different people under different conditions. Use the guide to identify the question to take to the responsible institution.

  • You are comparing university application rounds. Distinguish susi from the exam-based round, then check the university's current prospectus and acceptance rules.
  • A major comes with a company sponsor. Separate hiring-conditional from employee-training departments before applying the seat cap or interpreting a hiring promise.
  • You will spend time outside Korea. Separate suspension of benefits from exemption of premiums, and ask NHIS which conditions apply to your status.

Where the final answer lives: the university's admissions office and agreement, and the National Health Insurance Service's decision on your case.
