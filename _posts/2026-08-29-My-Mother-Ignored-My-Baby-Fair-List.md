<p><strong>Field note:</strong> adapted from the Korean diary entry I published on 27 April 2024, about our self-funded visit two days earlier. Entry, coupons, exhibitors and prices change between editions; the organiser's current COEX page is linked below rather than reconstructed from my old receipt.</p>

<p>This was my second baby fair, and the difference between the two visits was almost entirely preparation. The first time I wandered in curious and came out tired. This time I arrived heavily pregnant with a written list, and left having bought roughly what I meant to buy.</p>

<p>I did not strictly need to go. But my mother offered to buy things for the baby, which is a difficult offer to decline, so we went together.</p>

<h3>I Prepared for the Building, Not for My Mother</h3>

<p>The first visit had taught me enough to solve the dull part. In April 2024 I booked valet parking through a Kakao app and used a tower beside COEX. The main car park was already full when we arrived, so handing over the car and walking inside felt like a victory. That was our arrangement then, not current parking advice.</p>

<p>The person I had not planned around was my mother. I brought her partly for advice. She had not raised a baby in a long time and showed almost no interest in modern equipment. What she did have was a complete loss of composure whenever she saw tiny clothes.</p>

<h3>The 2024 Entry Routine</h3>

<p>That edition sent a pre-entry barcode through KakaoTalk. I scanned it at one of several kiosks, wore the printed sticker and collected the available coupon booklets inside. Some limited coupons were already gone. I had the list, not the timing, so I let those go.</p>

<p>Before shopping I stopped to put on the belly band I had doubted after the first fair. By late pregnancy I no longer doubted it.</p>

<!--post-ad-break-->

<h3>The Grandmother Effect</h3>

<p>I would ask about something practical and she would already be three booths away holding up an outfit. She compared the prices with department-store baby brands; I compared them with cheaper shops. Since she was the person offering to buy them, her comparison won.</p>

<p>We left the clothing area with several bodysuits, a pair of colic trousers and a traditional newborn <em>benet</em> suit. None had appeared on my written list.</p>

<h3>What Was on the List, and What I Actually Got</h3>

<p>My list was short: baby bottles, a bath tap fitting, a cheap waterproof mat, a bottle brush, and nail clippers and cotton buds.</p>

<p>The bottles were the main objective. Mothers I trusted told me that their babies had accepted this model, which was all the reassurance I had without a baby available to test it. We already had two small bottles from an online deal, so I bought a six-bottle set: two small with teats, four large without, plus one spare teat in the size we did not already own.</p>

<p>The waterproof mat was the easiest decision of the day. There were many booths selling them in bamboo, cotton, single-sided, double-sided and silicone. I bought the cheapest simple option: 10,000 won single-sided, 18,000 double-sided. I took the double.</p>

<p>The nail care and cotton bud brand was one I had skipped at the first fair, where its booth was enormous and impenetrable. On a weekday it was a modest stand I could simply walk into, so I picked up a nail care set and an assortment of cotton buds with the plan of trying them and only rebuying whatever we liked.</p>

<h3>The Purchase I Would Take Back</h3>

<p>The bath tap fitting is the honest failure of the day. I bought one brand; afterwards, experienced mothers in my chat told me a different brand was better. My husband and I were happy enough with ours, so I decided to just use it.</p>

<p>The genuine mistake was a matching holder accessory, too small for our shower fitting. I bought it without checking what we had at home. If something has to attach to existing plumbing, measure first.</p>

<p>My mother liked the demonstration so much that we bought extra sets for my parents and fitted them on the way home.</p>

<h3>The Receipt Versus the List</h3>

<p>The total came to 346,000 won across bottles, tap fittings, the nail and cotton-bud sets, clothing, the <em>benet</em> suit and the waterproof pad.</p>

<p>My list kept the practical shopping from spreading. It did nothing about a grandmother with a tiny outfit in each hand. Then we ate fast food, I dropped her home, and that was the day. Thanks, Mum.</p>
