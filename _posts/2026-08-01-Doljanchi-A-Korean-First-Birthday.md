<p>돌잔치 (doljanchi) is a celebration of a child's first birthday. The symbolic table, clothing and choosing ritual have a documented cultural history, but the size and format are a family decision. This is a cultural guide, not an account of our child's completed first-birthday party.</p>


<h3>Why the First Birthday Carries So Much Weight</h3>

<p>The Academy of Korean Studies' <em>Dolsang</em> entry connects the importance of the first birthday with historically high infant mortality. Reaching a year was a milestone worth celebrating, and the table expressed hopes for the child's continued health and future.</p>

<p>The Academy's <em>Dol</em> entry describes relatives and family acquaintances attending with gifts and sharing the food. That historical account helps explain the gathering's community aspect; it does not prescribe a guest list for a family today.</p>


<h3>백일 (baegil) Comes First</h3>

<p>백일 (baegil), the hundredth-day celebration, is a separate milestone before the first birthday. It should not be confused with 돌잔치, and a family's arrangements for one do not establish what they did for the other.</p>

<p>The traditional marker is 백설기 (baekseolgi), a steamed rice cake of little more than rice flour, sugar and water. It is deliberately white and deliberately plain, standing for purity, and families historically handed it round to neighbours so the good wishes were spread rather than hoarded. Alongside it comes 수수팥떡 (susu patteok), a sorghum and red-bean cake, red being the colour long thought to turn misfortune away.</p>


<p>For a documented family example, our <a href="/journal/A-Hundred-Day-Celebration-We-Held-At-Home-Twice/">hundredth-day record</a> describes two celebrations at home. The separate <a href="/journal/We-Booked-A-First-Birthday-Venue-At-Two-Months/">first-birthday venue record</a> describes a consultation and booking, not the eventual party.</p>


<h3>The 돌상 (dolsang), and What Is On It</h3>

<p>The centrepiece of a 돌잔치 is the 돌상 (dolsang), the first-birthday table. It is arranged rather than merely laid, and nearly everything on it carries a meaning.</p>

<ul>
<li>백설기 (baekseolgi): plain white rice cake, associated with purity.</li>
<li>수수팥떡 (susu patteok) &mdash; red sorghum and adzuki cake, to keep bad luck off.</li>
<li>쌀 (ssal) &mdash; a bowl of uncooked rice, for never going hungry.</li>
<li>대추 (daechu) &mdash; dried jujubes, associated with a large family and many descendants.</li>
<li>실타래 (siltarae) &mdash; skeins of thread used among the doljabi objects, for length of days.</li>
<li>Fruit: the <em>Dolsang</em> entry associates fruit and jujubes with descendants prospering.</li>
</ul>

<p>The Academy's account also includes 미역국 (miyeokguk), seaweed soup, in the first-birthday meal. A historical menu is useful context, not a promise about what a present-day host will serve. Ask about the actual menu if allergies or dietary needs matter.</p>


<h3>돌잡이 (doljabi): the Part Everyone Watches</h3>

<p>돌잡이 (doljabi) is the choosing ritual. Objects are placed in front of the child, and the items the child picks are interpreted symbolically as wishes or signs for the future. The objects and the way the family presents them can vary.</p>

<p>A core set of traditional objects recurs, though families and event vendors vary the selection. 실 (sil), thread, represents long life; 쌀 (ssal), rice, freedom from hunger; and 돈 (don), money, wealth. A 붓 (but), a calligraphy brush, or a 책 (chaek), a book, points to scholarship &mdash; historically a major ambition because passing the state examinations opened a route into office. Older sets could also include a 활 (hwal), a bow, associated with a military career, or a 자 (ja), a ruler, associated with domestic skill. These are less common in contemporary displays.</p>

<p>A present-day set may add objects for occupations or interests. If the host or vendor uses a different selection, ask what those objects mean in that celebration rather than treating one commercial set as the historic standard. The ritual is a cultural practice, not evidence that a child's career or lifespan can be predicted.</p>

<h3>돌복 (dolbok), the Clothes</h3>

<p>The child wears 돌복 (dolbok), first-birthday clothing based on 한복 (hanbok) but cut small and made comfortable enough to survive an afternoon. Girls typically wear a 저고리 (jeogori) jacket over a 치마 (chima) skirt, often with a 조바위 (jobawi) headpiece; boys wear a 저고리 with 바지 (baji) trousers and sometimes a 복건 (bokgeon) cap.</p>

<p>Clothing is one of the practical decisions to make when planning. Check whether it is included in a venue package, whether a fitting is needed and what the rental covers. A change of clothes can be useful, but neither rental nor a costume change is a requirement of the occasion.</p>


<h3>How a Modern 돌잔치 Actually Runs</h3>

<p>A hired room and an at-home celebration involve different planning questions. A venue may offer a table, decorations, food or a host, but the package needs checking item by item. In our <a href="/journal/We-Booked-A-First-Birthday-Venue-At-Two-Months/">published booking record</a>, food and photography were still separate decisions after the consultation.</p>

<p>Photography and a 성장 동영상 (seongjang dongyeongsang), a growth video, can be part of the plan. They are optional arrangements, not a checklist that every Korean family completes. Confirm permissions before sharing pictures of children or guests.</p>

<p>The Academy's historical account lists gifts including money, rice, thread, utensils and rings. That record does not establish a required present or spending amount today. If invited, ask the host or a close mutual relative what would be welcome rather than assuming an expensive gift is expected.</p>


<h3>A Planning Checklist, Not a Family Event Report</h3>

<ul>
<li><strong>People and place:</strong> choose the guest list before comparing room capacities.</li>
<li><strong>Package boundaries:</strong> list what the table, clothing, food and photography prices actually include.</li>
<li><strong>The child's needs:</strong> ask about a quiet place to rest, changing facilities and a manageable schedule.</li>
<li><strong>Terms:</strong> retain the agreed date, time, cancellation terms and supplier details.</li>
</ul>

<h3>If You Are Invited to One</h3>

<p>Follow the invitation for the time, location and dress expectations. Ask before bringing extra guests or a gift whose cost feels uncertain. If a meal is included, the <a href="/journal/Korean-Dining-Customs-Explained/">guide to Korean dining customs</a> provides useful background.</p>

<p>During 돌잡이, follow the host's cues and give the child space. Ask before taking or sharing photographs. The best preparation is understanding what the family has planned, not assuming every celebration follows one script.</p>
