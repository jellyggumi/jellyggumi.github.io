<p>The baby was two months old when we booked her first-birthday venue. It felt wildly early right up until another family asked for the same date that evening.</p>

<p><strong>Field note:</strong> adapted from our Korean write-up of an August 2024 visit. We received no benefit for the booking or this article. Capacity, timings and partner suppliers change, and the venue had only just opened; this is the decision record we kept, not a current listing.</p>

<p>A <em>doljanchi</em> (돌잔치) is the celebration of a Korean child's first birthday. It is still treated as a real event rather than a party — a decorated table, formal clothing, a growth video, and the <em>doljabi</em>, where the child picks an object said to hint at their future.</p>

<p>What nobody tells you is that the venues go first. We booked ours when INa was about two months old, and I still felt late.</p>

<h3>Why We Started So Early</h3>

<p>I kept reading that families began a year in advance, which sounded absurd until I looked at the limited slots in small rooms. A birthday can move to a nearby weekend; the supply of private rooms cannot.</p>

<p>I found the place we chose on Instagram. It had only recently opened, the scale looked right for what we had in mind, and I sent a direct message to arrange a consultation. Visits were by appointment only and only on Sundays, which is worth knowing if you are planning a scouting trip around a baby's schedule.</p>

<h3>Our Checklist Before the Visit</h3>

<p>Writing the criteria down before looking at any photographs was the single most useful thing we did. Pretty venues are easy to find; venues that match the event you actually want are not. Ours came to this:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Family only.</strong> We were inviting relatives from both sides — eight adults and two children besides ourselves — and did not want a space built for a crowd.</li>
  <li><strong>A cost we would not resent.</strong> Not the cheapest option, but nothing that would make the day feel like an expense to justify.</li>
  <li><strong>Close to home.</strong> A first birthday is already a demanding day for the child. A long journey either side of it is an avoidable problem.</li>
  <li><strong>Parking for at least five cars.</strong></li>
  <li><strong>One place for the table, photography and clothing.</strong> We had no appetite for coordinating four suppliers.</li>
  <li><strong>A traditional feel.</strong> Ideally a hanok or something close to it, rather than a generic banquet room.</li>
  <li><strong>Unhurried.</strong> Enough time that one family's session would not run into the next.</li>
</ul>

<p>The room was a short drive from home, which satisfied the criterion I cared about most. We arrived early for the appointment and walked through the door with the list.</p>

<h3>What the Consultation Actually Covered</h3>

<p>The owner walked us through the package, explained the space and answered the list. These were the parts that determined our decision.</p>

<p><strong>Capacity, and the honest number.</strong> The room held up to twenty, but the comfortable number was about twelve — the people who fit at the table already set. Extra tables were possible, but they sit in the gap people use to reach the food, and that gap is what stops a small room feeling cramped. One table was enough for us.</p>

<p><strong>Parking, in practice.</strong> There was no dedicated car park. Up to seven cars could park on the street in front, helped by the corner position, and the venue said they would hold the spaces the day before. That is a small operational detail that tells you a lot about how a place is run.</p>

<p><strong>Exclusive use, two slots a day.</strong> The hall runs two four-hour sessions. Four hours for a family lunch is generous, meaning no overlap with another family and no sense of being rushed. This was the point that decided it.</p>

<p><strong>The table itself.</strong> The folding screen, props and flowers landed in the narrow band between sparse and overloaded. Smaller arrangements continued along the guest table.</p>

<p><strong>The space behind the screen.</strong> A smaller area held traditional furniture and a bed that could work for photographs or a nap. There was also a nappy-changing table, storage for the fitted hanbok and a toilet inside the unit.</p>

<p><strong>What the package included and what it did not.</strong> The package covered the table, a photo table, a growth video, invitations, blessing cards, the doljabi objects, model rice cakes and fruit, a cake, and an MC. Photography, hanbok, food and hair and make-up could come through partner suppliers or be arranged independently. The hanbok partner ran fittings in advance and then rented the fitted garment for the day, which appealed to me because I like hanbok more than is strictly reasonable.</p>

<h3>The Part We Had Not Solved</h3>

<p>Food was the open question, and it stayed open after we signed.</p>

<p>The space had no kitchen. At our consultation the partner options were box catering or a visiting Italian buffet, and we could also bring our own food and drink. My original diary entry was later updated to note that the venue had added more catering partners, which is exactly why the 2024 list should not be treated as current.</p>

<p>I minded this more than the cost. Only family were coming, and they were making time to celebrate a one-year-old. The food did not need to be expensive, but it needed to look like somebody had thought about it. I left that for later, which is an honest way of saying I had not decided.</p>

<h3>How Fast the Date Went</h3>

<p>The payment structure was clear: hall hire, the table and the hanbok rental were paid up front, with food, photography and hair and make-up settled later. We saved the cancellation terms the venue published with the booking.</p>

<p>We contracted in August 2024 for a date in early June the following year, and I left saying we would confirm the morning or afternoon slot shortly. Her birthday is a public holiday and fell on a Friday, so the question was whether to use the day itself or move the party to the weekend.</p>

<p>That same evening the owner messaged: somebody else was asking about the date. We settled it by the following day and took the afternoon slot.</p>

<p>That same evening another family asked about the date. We chose the afternoon by the following day. The baby was two months old; the first-birthday slot was already real.</p>
