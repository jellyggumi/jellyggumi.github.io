<p>The tournament name 삼성화재배 월드바둑마스터스 (Samsung Cup World Baduk Masters) appeared in Korea's official Trending Now RSS snapshot on 31 August 2026. The durable story is not the spike. Korea publishes a dedicated Go Promotion Act and designates 5 November as Baduk Day. The Korea Baduk Association publishes daily professional results and maintains league sections, Baduk TV and the monthly magazine 월간바둑. The language section below checks every board-word claim against the Standard Korean Dictionary rather than treating familiar metaphors as automatic baduk history.</p>

<p>This guide walks through that institution layer by layer — the statute, the professionals, the language — using the primary sources themselves: the law as published by the National Law Information Center, the Standard Korean Dictionary, and the live pages of the Korea Baduk Association.</p>

<h3>A Board Game With Its Own Statute</h3>

<p>Start with the fact that sounds invented but is not. Korea's current Go Promotion Act (바둑 진흥법) is published as Act No. 18010, the amended text in force since 14 July 2021, and is administered by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism. Article 1 says the law aims to promote baduk and build the foundations of baduk culture for leisure, healthy mental cultivation and the globalization of the game.</p>

<p>The statute is short, but almost every article says something revealing about how seriously the state takes the game. Article 7 of the Act designates 5 November of every year as Baduk Day. Article 5 obliges the culture minister to establish a national baduk promotion basic plan every five years. Article 2 defines a baduk instructor (바둑지도자) as a holder of the baduk-discipline sport-instructor qualification under Article 11 of the National Sports Promotion Act — this definition ties baduk instruction to the sport-instructor system. Article 2 defines a professional player (바둑전문기사) as someone who passed a skill-verification competition prescribed by presidential decree. Even the future is legislated: Article 12 tells the culture minister to reflect convergence with traditional play, artificial intelligence and science and technology in related policy.</p>

<h3>The Professional Machine</h3>

<p>The working centre of Korean baduk is 한국기원 (Hanguk Kiwon, the Korea Baduk Association). Its front page reads less like a heritage site and more like a sports channel. The listed structure includes a Korean Baduk League, a women's league, a legends league and a challenge league, with team standings, MVP awards and championship finals.</p>

<p>The trending tournament is part of the same machinery. On 30 August 2026, Mok Jinseok passed the 2026 Samsung Cup World Baduk Masters integrated preliminary as the only Korean player to do so. The association reports the qualification result in the same results-and-leagues system it uses for professional competition.</p>

<h3>The Vocabulary That Escaped the Board</h3>

<p>Here is a layer that is easy to miss in translation. The dictionary records board and non-board senses term by term. In the Standard Korean Dictionary, 정석(定石) carries both the everyday sense of the fixed way of doing something and, as its second sense, the baduk sense of the established best sequence of stones. That shared headword links the ordinary and baduk senses, although the entry does not say that every speaker consciously invokes the game.</p>

<p>In the Standard Korean Dictionary, the first listed sense of 초읽기 is the baduk countdown in which a recorder calls out the final seconds of a player's clock. The same entry's second sense defines it figuratively as a situation becoming urgent for time. That official second sense, not a list of invented examples, is the evidence that the word travelled beyond the board.</p>

<p>Once the pattern is visible, it keeps appearing, and the dictionary keeps receipts. The Standard Korean Dictionary defines 승부수(勝負手) as a decisive move that determines the outcome in baduk or similar board games. The Standard Korean Dictionary lists the first sense of 무리수(無理手) as a baduk move played out of overreach. The Standard Korean Dictionary defines 자충수(自充手) first as the baduk move that fills in one's own liberties. Those board definitions are the starting point for reading later metaphors; the immediate context, not the syllable 수 alone, determines whether a speaker is invoking the game.</p>

<p>One honest wrinkle, because the boundary is fuzzier than it looks. The Standard Korean Dictionary's entry for 꼼수 records only the general sense of a petty trick, with no baduk label. So not every syllable of 수 in Korean cunning traces back to the board — some of the vocabulary only sounds like it walked out of a game.</p>

<h3>Meeting the Game Without Playing It</h3>

<p>Baduk is an accessible layer of Korean culture even before a reader learns the rules. The Korea Baduk Association publishes daily result pages and Baduk TV programming listings; the museum and cultural-centre photographs below document physical boards and stones in their credited source records.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>On screen.</strong> The association's site lists each day's professional results and fixtures alongside its Baduk TV programming. Even without Korean, the board and the clock speak for themselves.</li>
<li><strong>In museums and cultural venues.</strong> The credited Commons record places stone pieces in the National Museum of Korea's Sinan Shipwreck Gallery. A Wikimedia Commons record describes a Korean baduk board and carries the object name “Ansan Cultural Center 62.”</li>

Those move at the speed of law and lexicography, which is to say, slowly.</p>
